Retinoids 101: A Dermatologist's Guide to Getting Started

Dr. Aisha Rahman, MD, FAAD · 7 min read

Almost every patient who asks about retinoids has already tried one — usually for a week, usually with some peeling, and usually followed by putting the tube in a drawer. That's not a failure of the product. It's what happens when a genuinely effective ingredient gets used without a plan.

What a retinoid actually does

Retinoids are vitamin A derivatives that speed up cell turnover and increase collagen production. That's the mechanism behind almost everything people want from them: fewer breakouts, smoother texture, and fewer fine lines over time. It's also why the first few weeks can be rough — your skin's surface layer is renewing faster than it's used to.

The irritation isn't a sign it's working harder. It's a sign you started too fast.

The routine that actually holds up

Here's what we tell patients in the exam room, not the aggressive version you'll find in a five-star review:

  • Start at two nights a week. Not nightly. Give your skin a full week to adjust before adding a third night.
  • Buffer it. Apply a plain moisturizer first, then the retinoid over top, especially for the first month.
  • Pea-sized amount, full face. More product doesn't mean faster results — it means more irritation.
  • Sunscreen the next morning, no exceptions. Retinoid-treated skin is more sensitive to UV.

When to actually worry

Mild flaking and a bit of redness in week two or three is expected. Persistent burning, swelling, or irritation that doesn't settle after a week off is not — that's when it's worth a visit rather than pushing through.

How long until you see something

Realistically: eight to twelve weeks for texture and breakouts, and three to six months for any visible change in fine lines. If a routine promises faster than that, the "results" are usually just less irritation, not more collagen.

If you've tried retinoids before and given up, that's a completely reasonable thing to bring to a visit — we adjust strength, frequency, and formulation based on what specifically didn't work the first time.
